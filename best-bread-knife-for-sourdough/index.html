What makes a good bread knife

  • Length. A 9–10" blade spans a round boule so you can saw with long, easy strokes instead of hacking.
  • Serrations. Fewer, deeper, pointed serrations grab a hard crust; very fine serrations give cleaner slices of soft bread and tomatoes.
  • Grip. Crusty sourdough takes real pressure; a grippy handle with knuckle clearance keeps your fingers off the board.

Slicing tips

  • Wait. Let the loaf cool at least an hour; the inside is still setting when it leaves the oven and slicing early makes it gummy.
  • Turn it on its side. Slicing a boule on its side makes the crust easier to start.
  • Let the blade work. Long, light sawing strokes beat pushing down.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of knife is best for cutting sourdough?

A long serrated bread knife. The teeth bite through a hard crust without crushing the soft crumb.

How long should a bread knife be for sourdough?

Longer than your loaf is wide, so you can saw in long strokes. A blade of about 10 inches suits most home loaves.

When should I slice sourdough after baking?

Wait at least an hour, ideally until it is fully cool. The crumb is still setting as it cools, and cutting early makes it gummy.

Is an electric knife good for sourdough?

Some bakers like them for even slices, but a good manual serrated knife does the job for most people and takes less space.
