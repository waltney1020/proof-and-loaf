How we choose sourdough gear

The honest version: our recommendations come from research, not from a test kitchen. Here is exactly how we pick, so you can decide how much weight to give them.

1. Start with a real beginner question

Each guide answers one specific question a new baker asks, like “Do I need a proofing box?” or “What size jar does a starter need?” If the honest answer is “use what you already have,” we say that first.

2. Check the specs that matter for sourdough

We read manufacturer specifications for the things that actually affect bread: oven-safe temperature (including lid knobs), capacity, dimensions, weight, materials and care requirements. Where a spec is a manufacturer claim, we say “the listing says” or “per the manufacturer.”

3. Look at independent testing

When respected testers have put products through structured tests, such as America’s Test Kitchen, Serious Eats or Wirecutter, we note their results and name them in the guide. We don’t copy their rankings; we use them as evidence.

4. Read long-term owner feedback

We look for patterns in what owners and experienced bakers report over months of use: enamel chipping, handles getting too hot, baskets molding, blades dulling. Recurring problems go in the “Watch out for” list.

5. Explain the tradeoffs, including skipping it

Every pick gets a plain-English “Why we like it” and “Watch out for.” We include a budget option and a no-buy alternative whenever there is one.

What we never do

  • Claim hands-on testing we haven’t done, or invent test results.
  • Publish star ratings, scores or review markup.
  • Accept payment or free products in exchange for coverage or placement.
  • Let commission rates decide what we recommend. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases; see our affiliate disclosure.
  • Show prices, which change constantly. The price on Amazon is the one that applies.

Keeping guides current

Each guide shows the date it was last updated. We re-check product links and listings periodically and update a guide when a product changes, is discontinued or a better-supported option appears.
