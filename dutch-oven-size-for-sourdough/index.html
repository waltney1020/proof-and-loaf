Short version: a 5 to 6 quart round pot fits the standard one-loaf recipe. Here’s how to size for smaller loaves, ovals and the pot you already own.

How we chose: we compared manufacturer specs, independent test-kitchen results and owner feedback. We haven’t hands-on tested these products ourselves. Our methodology.

Quick answer

  • Standard loaf (about 500 g flour, 900–1,000 g dough), round boule: a 5 to 6 quart round Dutch oven.
  • Same dough shaped as an oval batard: a 6 to 7 quart oval.
  • Smaller loaves (300–400 g flour): 3.5 to 4.5 quarts is plenty.
  • Combo cookers are the exception: the Lodge Combo Cooker is sold as 3.2 qt, but you bake on its wide skillet lid under the deep pot, so a standard boule fits.

These ranges are a starting point drawn from manufacturer sizing and common baker guidance, not lab measurements. Shape, hydration and how tightly you shape all change how much a loaf spreads.

How to check a pot you already own

Put your empty banneton (or the bowl you proof in) inside the cold pot. If it fits with roughly an inch to spare all around, your dough will fit. Also check the depth: about 4 inches or more gives the loaf room to rise without touching the lid.

Too small or too big: what happens

  • Too small: the loaf presses against the hot walls, the sides scorch and oven spring is limited. Parchment can crease into the crust.
  • Too big: it still works. Steam is a little less concentrated and the pot is heavier to handle, but a well-shaped loaf holds its shape in the middle. When in doubt, size up rather than down.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 4 quart Dutch oven big enough for sourdough?

For smaller loaves (about 300 to 400 g of flour), yes. For a standard 500 g-flour loaf it is tight; 5 to 6 quarts is more forgiving.

Is a 7 quart Dutch oven too big for sourdough?

No. A well-shaped loaf holds its shape in a bigger pot. It is mainly heavier to lift, and steam is slightly less concentrated.

Round or oval Dutch oven for sourdough?

Round for boules, oval for batards. If you only buy one, a round 5 to 6 quart pot is the most versatile.

Why does the 3.2 quart combo cooker fit a full loaf?

You bake on its wide, shallow skillet lid and cover the loaf with the deep pot, so the usable space is larger than the quart rating suggests.
