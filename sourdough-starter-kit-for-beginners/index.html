The short list

  • Must have: a digital scale, a jar for your starter, and a way to bake with steam (a Dutch oven or a covered tray).
  • Very helpful: a banneton, a bench scraper, a lame, an instant-read thermometer.
  • Nice extras: a dough whisk and one good bread book.

One good book

A recipe from a website gets you a loaf; a good book teaches you why it worked. Two classics many bakers start with: Flour Water Salt Yeast by Ken Forkish (clear schedules and techniques for home bakers) and Tartine Bread by Chad Robertson (the book that popularized the open-crumb country loaf).

What you can skip

  • Starter “kits” built around dried starter. Flour and water make a starter in about one to two weeks, or a baker friend can give you some.
  • Special flour sifters, cooling racks and storage boxes. Your oven rack works as a cooling rack.
  • A stand mixer. Most sourdough recipes use gentle stretch-and-folds by hand.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum equipment to make sourdough?

A digital scale, a jar for your starter, a mixing bowl, and a way to trap steam while baking (a Dutch oven, or a tray covered with an upside-down roasting pan). Everything else makes it easier but isn't essential.

Should I buy an all-in-one sourdough starter kit?

Bundled kits can be convenient, especially as a gift, but they rarely include a scale or a baking vessel, which matter most. Check what is inside before buying, and skip duplicates of things you already own.

Do I need a banneton to make sourdough?

No. A bowl or colander lined with a well-floured tea towel works. A banneton just makes shaping and turning out easier and leaves the classic ring pattern.

Do I need a stand mixer for sourdough?

No. Most sourdough recipes use stretch-and-folds by hand, which takes a few minutes spread over a couple of hours.
