A jar of your own starter is one of the best homemade gifts a baker can give. Here’s how to pack it so it arrives alive, and what to write on the card.

Quick answer

  • Feed your starter, then pack about 50–100 g of it at or just before its peak into a clean jar with room to double.
  • Leave the lid loose (or use cloth and a band), add a label, and tuck in a feeding instruction card.
  • Hand it over within a day, or tell them to refrigerate it right away and feed it within a few days.
  • Mailing it or giving it weeks ahead? Give dried starter flakes in a small sealed jar or bag instead.

Why starter makes such a good gift

A spoonful of healthy starter skips the one- to two-week wait of building one from flour and water, which is the step where many beginners give up. It costs you almost nothing (it’s the portion you’d discard anyway), and pairing it with one small tool turns it into a thoughtful gift for a new baker.

Step 1: Get the starter ready

  1. Feed it the day before. Give your starter one or two regular feedings so it’s lively and predictable.
  2. Feed again a few hours before you pack it. Pack it when it has risen and is bubbly, or slightly before the peak, so it doesn’t run out of food on the way.
  3. Portion it. 50–100 g is plenty: the recipient only needs a little to start feeding. Keep the rest for yourself.

Step 2: Choose the jar

Active starter makes gas and can double, so the jar needs space and a lid that isn’t sealed tight. Small, wide-mouth glass jars are the easiest.

Step 3: Label it

A handwritten tag or a strip of masking tape is enough. Include:

  • What it is (“Live sourdough starter”) and the date you packed it.
  • “Keep the lid loose” and “Refrigerate if not feeding within a day.”
  • The flour you feed it (for example bread flour, or a bread and whole wheat mix) and the ratio you use.
  • Optional: the starter’s name. Many bakers name theirs, and it makes the gift more fun.

Step 4: Write the instruction card

Copy this, adjust it to how you feed your own starter, and print or handwrite it:

Your sourdough starter: how to keep it alive

  1. Today or tomorrow: stir it, discard all but about 50 g, and add 50 g flour and 50 g lukewarm water. Mix, mark the level with a rubber band, and cover loosely.
  2. Repeat every 12–24 hours at room temperature. It’s ready to bake with when it reliably doubles within several hours of feeding and smells pleasantly tangy.
  3. Not baking this week? Feed it, let it sit an hour, then keep it in the fridge. Feed it about once a week.
  4. Sluggish after the trip? That’s normal. Two or three feedings in a warm spot usually bring it back.
  5. Liquid on top (“hooch”) means it’s hungry: pour it off or stir it in, then feed. Pink, orange or fuzzy mold means start over.

Giving it weeks ahead or mailing it? Dry it

Live starter in a jar is happiest delivered in person. For mailing or for a gift you’re packing well ahead, dried starter travels far better:

  1. Spread a thin layer of active starter on parchment or a silicone mat.
  2. Let it dry completely at room temperature, away from heat, which can take a few days.
  3. Break it into flakes, seal them in a small, completely dry jar or zip bag, and label it.
  4. On the card, tell them to revive it by mixing the flakes with a little water, then adding flour, and feeding daily until it’s bubbly again. Expect a few days.

Drying and reviving times vary with your kitchen and flour; treat these as typical ranges from common baker practice, not exact numbers.

Gift timing for the holidays

  • Live starter: pack it the day you give it. If you’re traveling, keep it cool in a bag with an ice pack and let them know to feed it on arrival.
  • Dried starter: make it in early December; it keeps well while dry and sealed.
  • Tools to go with it: order by early December so shipping isn’t a worry.

Frequently asked questions

How much sourdough starter should I give as a gift?

About 50 to 100 g is plenty. The recipient only needs a small amount to begin regular feedings, and a small portion fits an 8 oz jar with room to rise.

How long can sourdough starter stay out as a gift?

Freshly fed starter is fine at room temperature for about a day. After that it gets hungry, so tell the recipient to feed it or refrigerate it as soon as they get it.

Can you mail sourdough starter?

Dried starter flakes mail far better than live starter. Spread active starter thin on parchment, let it dry completely, crumble it and seal it in a dry jar or bag with revival instructions.

Should the lid be tight on a starter gift jar?

No. Active starter makes gas, so leave the lid loose or use cloth and a band. A tightly sealed jar can build pressure.

What do I write on a sourdough starter gift tag?

What it is, the date, the flour and ratio you feed it, and simple care steps: feed within a day or refrigerate, keep the lid loose, and feed weekly if stored in the fridge.
