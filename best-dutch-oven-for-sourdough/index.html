Why a Dutch oven makes such a difference

Home ovens are dry. A professional bakery injects steam during the first part of the bake so the outside of the loaf stays soft long enough for the bread to expand (“oven spring”) before the crust sets. A preheated, lidded pot does the same job at home: the dough releases moisture, the lid traps it, and the heavy walls deliver strong, even heat. Most recipes bake covered for the first 20 minutes or so, then uncovered to brown the crust.

What to look for

  • Size and shape. A round pot in the 5–6 quart range fits the typical one-loaf recipe (about 500 g of flour); smaller loaves are fine in 4 quarts, and oval pots of 6–7 quarts suit long batards. Our Dutch oven size guide has a full chart.
  • Loading. Lowering wobbly dough into a deep, 450°F+ pot is the scariest part for beginners. Designs where you bake on the shallow half (combo cookers, cloches) make this much easier and safer.
  • Heat rating. Sourdough is usually baked hot. Check that every part, especially the lid knob, is rated for the temperature your recipe uses.
  • Bare vs. enameled cast iron. Bare cast iron is cheap and nearly indestructible but needs to stay seasoned. Enamel is easier to clean, but long empty preheats at high heat can stain or wear the interior over time.

Can I skip the Dutch oven?

Yes. Bake on a preheated baking sheet or pizza stone and cover the loaf with an upside-down, oven-safe roasting pan or large stainless bowl for the first 20 minutes. Results are usually a little flatter, but it is a fine way to try sourdough before buying anything. See how to bake sourdough without a Dutch oven for four methods.

Frequently asked questions

What size Dutch oven do I need for sourdough?

For a standard one-loaf recipe (about 500 g of flour), a 5 to 6 quart round pot works well for a boule, or a 6 to 7 quart oval for a batard. Combo cookers are the exception: you bake on the wide skillet lid, so the 3.2 qt Lodge fits a standard loaf.

Do I preheat the Dutch oven empty?

Most recipes do, usually for 30 to 45 minutes. Follow your pot's manual: some enameled brands advise against long empty preheats at the highest temperatures.

Is enameled or bare cast iron better for sourdough?

Both bake equally well. Bare cast iron is cheaper and tolerates very high heat but needs to stay seasoned. Enamel is easier to clean but can stain or wear with repeated empty preheats, and some lid knobs have lower heat ratings.

How do I stop the bottom of my sourdough burning?

Put a baking sheet on the rack under the pot, or move the pot up a rack, for the uncovered part of the bake. A little cornmeal or rice flour under the parchment also helps.

Do I need parchment paper in a Dutch oven?

It isn't required, but a parchment sling makes lowering the dough into a hot pot much safer and keeps it from sticking. Most beginners find it worth using.
