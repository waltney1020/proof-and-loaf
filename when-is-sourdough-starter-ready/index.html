Quick answer

  • Use it at or near its peak: usually when it has roughly doubled (some starters rise less), is bubbly all the way through and has a domed or just-flattening top.
  • Typical timing: many starters peak about 4–12 hours after feeding, depending on kitchen temperature and feeding ratio.
  • The float test is optional. It can help with a thick white-flour starter, but wet or whole-grain starters often sink even when they’re ready.
  • A rubber band or tape on the jar is the easiest way to see how far it has risen.

What “ready” actually means

A starter is ready to bake with when its yeast is at its most active. After a feeding it grows, peaks, then slowly falls as it runs out of food. Bread rises best when you mix the dough with starter taken around that peak, or a little before or after it. A brand-new starter is a different question: most need one to two weeks of regular feeding before they rise predictably at all.

5 signs your sourdough starter is ready

  1. It rises predictably. It grows by a similar amount, often around double, within a similar time after each feeding. One lucky rise isn’t enough; look for a pattern over two or three days.
  2. Bubbles throughout. Look through the side of the jar: bubbles should run from top to bottom, not just sit on the surface.
  3. A domed or just-flattening top. A domed top means it’s still rising. A top that has just started to flatten or crinkle means it’s at or just past peak, which is still a good time to use it.
  4. A light, airy texture. Stir a spoon through it. It should feel stretchy and full of air, not soupy or heavy.
  5. A pleasant smell. Mildly sour, yeasty or a little like yogurt or beer. A sharp nail-polish smell usually means it’s hungry and past its peak, so feed it and try again.

Does the float test work?

The float test means dropping a spoonful of starter into water. If it floats, it’s full of gas and probably active. It’s a handy quick check for thick, white-flour starters. But plenty of healthy starters sink, especially wet (high-hydration) or whole-grain ones, and stirring the starter first knocks the gas out. Many bakers skip it and trust the rise, bubbles and timing instead.

How long after feeding is starter ready?

These are typical ranges bakers report for a healthy, established starter fed 1:1:1. A bigger feed (like 1:5:5) or a cold kitchen pushes the peak later; a warm kitchen brings it sooner.

Kitchen temperatureTypical time to peak
Warm, about 78–80°F (26–27°C)About 3–6 hours
Average, about 70–75°F (21–24°C)About 4–8 hours
Cool, about 65°F (18°C) or belowAbout 8–12 hours or longer

Want it ready at a certain time, such as first thing on a holiday baking morning? Adjust the feeding ratio. Our feeding ratio guide explains how 1:2:2 or 1:5:5 slows the peak so it lands when you want it.

How to track it the easy way

  1. Feed it in a straight-sided, clear jar.
  2. Mark the level with a rubber band, a strip of tape or a dry-erase marker, and note the time.
  3. Check every couple of hours. Write down when it peaks. After two or three feedings you’ll know its rhythm.
  4. Weigh, don’t scoop. Feeding by weight keeps the timing consistent from day to day.

What if it never seems ready?

A starter that rises only a little, or not at all, is usually cold, underfed or still too young. Our guide to sourdough starter not rising walks through eight common causes and free fixes. Starter coming out of the fridge? Give it one or two room-temperature feedings first; see storing starter in the fridge.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know my sourdough starter is ready?

It rises predictably after each feeding, often around double, has bubbles all the way through, a domed or just-flattening top, an airy texture and a pleasant sour or yeasty smell.

Does the float test work for sourdough starter?

Sometimes. A floating spoonful usually means it is active, but wet or whole-grain starters often sink even when ready, so trust the rise and bubbles more.

How long after feeding is sourdough starter ready?

Many established starters peak about 4 to 8 hours after a 1:1:1 feed at around 70 to 75F. Warmer kitchens are faster; cooler kitchens or bigger feeds are slower.

Can I use sourdough starter after it peaks?

Yes, shortly after. A starter that has just begun to fall still raises bread well. If it has collapsed and smells sharp, feed it and wait for the next peak.

How long before a new sourdough starter is ready?

Most new starters need about one to two weeks of regular feeding before they rise predictably enough to bake with.
