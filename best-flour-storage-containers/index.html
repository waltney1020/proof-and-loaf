Why flour storage matters for sourdough

Sourdough bakers go through flour fast and often keep two or three kinds (bread flour, whole wheat, rye). A good container keeps flour dry, keeps pantry pests out, and makes scooping onto the scale less messy. Whole-grain flours contain more oils and keep longer in the fridge or freezer, so a container that fits there is a plus.

What to look for

  • Size. Match the bag you buy: a 5 lb bag fits a container of roughly 4–6 quarts; 25 lb bulk bags need a large bin.
  • A wide mouth. You should be able to dip a scoop or measuring cup straight in.
  • A good seal. Airtight lids keep humidity and bugs out.
  • Clear sides. See at a glance when you’re running low.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to store flour in an airtight container?

It is a good idea. An airtight container keeps out moisture, odors and pantry pests, which paper flour bags don't.

What size container holds a 5 lb bag of flour?

A 5 lb bag is roughly 4 to 4.5 quarts of flour, so choose a container of at least 5 to 6 quarts to leave room for scooping.

How long does flour last in a container?

White flour keeps for months in a cool, dry pantry. Whole wheat and rye go stale faster because of their natural oils, so many bakers keep them in the fridge or freezer.

How do I keep bugs out of my flour?

Use airtight containers and wipe up spills. Some bakers freeze new bags for a few days before decanting.
