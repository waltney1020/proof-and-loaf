What matters in a bread scale

  • 1 g resolution. Bread recipes are written in grams; a scale that counts in 1 g steps is precise enough for flour, water and starter.
  • Capacity of 5 kg (11 lb) or more. A big mixing bowl can weigh over a kilo on its own, before any dough goes in.
  • A display you can read. Wide bowls can hide the screen. A pull-out or raised display solves this.
  • Tare. Lets you zero the scale with the bowl on it and add ingredients one after another.
  • Baker’s percentage (optional). Some scales can show each ingredient as a percentage of flour weight, which is how bread recipes are designed.

A tip for salt and yeast

Most kitchen scales are accurate to about a gram, which is fine for flour and water. If you bake small batches and want exact salt amounts, a separate 0.01 g pocket scale is a cheap add-on, but it isn’t necessary to bake good bread.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bake sourdough with cups instead of a scale?

You can, but results vary a lot because a cup of flour can weigh very different amounts depending on how it is scooped. A scale is the fastest way to consistent loaves.

What capacity scale do I need for bread?

Around 5 kg (11 lb) is a comfortable minimum. It covers flour, water and a heavy mixing bowl at once without maxing out.

Do I need a scale that measures 0.1 gram?

Not for flour and water. A 1 g scale is fine. If you bake small batches and want exact salt or yeast amounts, a cheap 0.01 g pocket scale is an optional extra.

Why does my scale turn off mid-recipe?

Many scales have an auto-off timer. Check the manual; some let you disable it or extend it.
