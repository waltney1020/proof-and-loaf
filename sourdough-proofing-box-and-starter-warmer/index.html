A cold kitchen is the most common reason a beginner’s starter stalls and loaves come out dense. Try these free fixes first, then decide whether a proofer is worth it.

Why temperature matters so much

Fermentation speed depends on temperature. In a warm kitchen a starter peaks quickly and dough can over-proof before you notice; in a cool kitchen everything crawls and the bread comes out dense and sour. Many bakers aim for a warm, steady spot (often quoted as roughly the mid-70s°F) because it makes timing predictable. You can get there for free, or buy a box that holds the temperature for you.

Free options to try first

  • Oven with only the light on. Many ovens get warm this way, but some get too warm. Measure with a thermometer before trusting it, and put a note on the door so nobody preheats with your dough inside.
  • Microwave with a mug of just-boiled water. A small, draft-free box that stays warm for a while. Replace the water when it cools.
  • On top of the fridge or near (not on) a warm appliance. Often a few degrees warmer than the rest of the kitchen.

When a proofing box is worth it
