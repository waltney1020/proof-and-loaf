A good bread book teaches you to fix problems, not just follow steps. Here are five classics and who each one is for.

Why a book beats a random recipe

Online recipes tell you what to do; good books explain why, so you can fix a dense loaf or adapt a schedule to your kitchen. These are widely recommended classics, ordered from most beginner-friendly to most in-depth.

Best for total beginners

Artisan Sourdough Made Simple by Emilie Raffa

A friendly, photo-heavy beginner’s guide with minimal kneading. The publisher lists 65 recipes.

Why we like it
  • Approachable and visual
  • Minimal-knead methods
Watch out for
  • Less depth on the science
Check price on Amazon
Best first “real” bread book

Flour Water Salt Yeast by Ken Forkish

Clear schedules for home bakers, with both yeasted and levain (sourdough) breads plus pizza. Many bakers learn their fundamentals here.

Why we like it
  • Excellent, flexible schedules
  • Covers pizza dough too
Watch out for
  • Recipes make large batches by default
Check price on Amazon
Best for going deep on sourdough

The Perfect Loaf by Maurizio Leo

A modern, thorough book on the craft and science of sourdough, from breads to sweets, by the baker behind The Perfect Loaf website.

Why we like it
  • Deep explanations
  • Wide range of recipes
Watch out for
  • Can feel like a lot at first
Check price on Amazon
The modern classic

Tartine Bread by Chad Robertson

The book that popularized the open-crumb country loaf at home. Inspiring, though the method is detailed.

Why we like it
  • Iconic country loaf method
  • Beautiful and inspiring
Watch out for
  • Long, demanding method for a first loaf
Check price on Amazon
For all kinds of bread

The Bread Baker’s Apprentice, 15th Anniversary Edition by Peter Reinhart

A broad bread education covering many styles, not only sourdough. Great for a baker who wants to branch out.

Why we like it
  • Wide range of breads
  • Strong technique explanations
Watch out for
  • Not sourdough-focused
