Quick answer

  • There’s no fixed time. Bulk fermentation is done when the dough looks right, not when a timer goes off. Temperature is the biggest reason times vary.
  • Rough guide: many recipes land around 4–6 hours in a warm kitchen near 78°F (26°C), and 8–12 hours or more in a cool one near 68°F (20°C).
  • Watch for: a rise of roughly 50–75% for most beginner recipes, a domed, jiggly dough, bubbles on the top and sides, and a smoother, airier feel.
  • Easiest tool: a straight-sided container with a mark at the starting level, so you can see the rise instead of guessing.

What bulk fermentation is

Bulk fermentation (often just “bulk”) is the first rise: the time from mixing the dough until you divide and shape it. The starter’s yeast makes gas that inflates the dough, the bacteria build flavor, and stretch-and-folds during the first couple of hours build strength. Most of a loaf’s final texture is decided here, which is why ending bulk too early is one of the most common causes of dense, gummy sourdough.

How long bulk takes at different temperatures

These are typical ranges reported by bakers and recipe writers for a dough using around 20% starter. Your flour, starter strength and recipe will shift them, so treat this as a way to plan your day, then let the dough have the final say.

Dough temperatureTypical bulk timeWhat to expect
82°F (28°C)About 3–5 hoursFast; check often so it doesn’t overshoot
78°F (26°C)About 4–6 hoursThe range many recipes are written for
72°F (22°C)About 6–8 hoursA comfortable working pace
68°F (20°C)About 8–12 hoursCommon in winter kitchens; easy to end too early
Below 65°F (18°C)12 hours or moreVery slow; consider a warmer spot

Dough temperature matters more than room temperature. A dough mixed with cold water in a warm room can stay cool for hours, so many bakers aim for a dough around 76–78°F by adjusting water temperature, and check it with an instant-read thermometer.

How to tell bulk fermentation is done

  1. It has risen. Most beginner recipes call for roughly 50–75% growth. Some recipes ask for less or more, so follow yours.
  2. The top is domed where it meets the container, not flat or sinking.
  3. You can see bubbles on the surface and through the sides of a clear container, of a few different sizes.
  4. It jiggles as one airy mass when you gently shake the container.
  5. It feels different. Compared with just after mixing, the dough is lighter, softer and less sticky, and it releases from the container more cleanly.

The “doubled” rule from yeasted bread is a common trap. Many sourdough doughs that double during bulk end up overproofed, especially when they will also have a long cold proof. If your loaves spread flat, read overproofed sourdough signs and fixes.

Underdone vs overdone bulk

Under-fermentedOver-fermented
DoughTight, dense, few bubblesSlack, very sticky, may smell boozy
ShapingHolds shape but feels heavyTears or won’t hold tension
Baked loafDense, gummy streaks, big tunnels under the crustFlat, pale, wide, with an even tight crumb

How to control bulk timing

  • Warm the dough with warmer mixing water, or move it somewhere warmer: an oven with just the light on (check it doesn’t get too hot), a spot near, but not on, a warm appliance, or a proofing box.
  • Slow it down with cooler water, or by using a little less starter in the recipe.
  • Use a strong starter. A starter used near its peak keeps bulk predictable. If yours is sluggish, start with sourdough starter not rising and the feeding ratio guide.
  • Write it down. Note dough temperature, start time and rise each bake. After two or three bakes you’ll know your kitchen’s rhythm.

Frequently asked questions

How long should sourdough bulk ferment?

It depends mostly on dough temperature. Many recipes land around 4 to 6 hours near 78F (26C) and 8 to 12 hours near 68F (20C). End it when the dough shows the signs, not at a set time.

How much should sourdough rise during bulk fermentation?

Most beginner recipes aim for roughly 50 to 75 percent growth. Doubling is often too far for sourdough, especially before a long cold proof.

Can you bulk ferment sourdough overnight?

Yes, in a cool kitchen or with less starter, but a warm kitchen can overferment it overnight. Many bakers instead do bulk in the day and the cold proof overnight in the fridge.

Does bulk fermentation include stretch and folds?

Yes. Bulk starts when you mix the dough, and stretch-and-folds usually happen in its first couple of hours.

How do I speed up bulk fermentation in a cold kitchen?

Mix with warmer water, then keep the dough somewhere warm, such as an oven with only the light on (check the temperature) or a proofing box.
