Quick answer

  • Typical window: many bakers cold proof shaped sourdough in the fridge for about 8–16 hours, and plenty of recipes stretch it to 24 hours or more.
  • Fridge temperature matters: around 38–40°F (3–4°C) keeps the dough nearly still; a warmer fridge keeps it rising, so shorten the time.
  • Bake it straight from the fridge. Cold dough is firmer, easier to score and holds its shape in a hot Dutch oven.
  • Great for holidays: mix and shape the day before, then bake fresh bread the morning you need it.

What cold proofing (retarding) does

Cold proofing, also called retarding, is the final rise done in the fridge instead of on the counter. The cold slows the yeast a lot and the bacteria a little less, so the dough keeps building flavor while barely growing. That gives you three things beginners love: a tangier, deeper-tasting loaf, a firm dough that’s easy to score, and a flexible bake time that fits around work, sleep or a holiday meal.

How long to cold proof sourdough

These are typical ranges reported by bakers and recipe writers. Your recipe, starter strength and how far bulk fermentation went all shift them, so treat this as a planning guide.

Time in the fridgeWhat bakers usually notice
2–6 hoursFirms the dough for scoring; mild flavor. Fine if you’re short on time.
8–16 hoursThe most common overnight window: good flavor, easy scoring, reliable spring.
16–24 hoursMore tang; still works well in a cold fridge if bulk wasn’t pushed too far.
24–48 hoursPossible in a very cold fridge with a shorter bulk; risk of overproofing rises.

The bigger risk is the dough going in already overproofed. If bulk ran long, keep the cold proof short. Our bulk fermentation guide shows when to stop bulk, and overproofed sourdough signs and fixes covers what to do if it went too far.

How to cold proof, step by step

  1. Finish bulk fermentation when the dough has risen roughly 50–75% and looks airy.
  2. Shape the loaf with good surface tension.
  3. Put it seam-side up in a floured banneton or a bowl lined with a well-floured tea towel. Rice flour helps stop sticking.
  4. Cover it with a bag, a shower cap or a plate so the surface doesn’t dry out and form a skin.
  5. Refrigerate on a shelf away from the door, where the temperature is steadier.
  6. Preheat the oven and pot about 45–60 minutes before baking, then turn the dough out, score and bake straight from the fridge.

Is it ready to bake? The poke test

Press a floured finger about half an inch into the cold dough. If the dent springs back slowly and only partly, it’s usually ready. If it springs back right away, it may need more time, either longer in the fridge or 30–60 minutes on the counter. If the dent stays put and the dough feels fragile, it’s likely overproofed: bake it soon, and expect a flatter loaf. Cold dough reacts more slowly than warm dough, so read the test a little generously.

Common cold-proof problems

  • Dough stuck to the basket: use more rice flour, or a liner. See banneton care.
  • Dry skin on top: cover it more tightly next time.
  • Spread flat when turned out: bulk probably went too far, the cold proof was too long for a warm fridge, or the shaping was loose.
  • Dense crumb: usually bulk ended too early; the fridge can’t make up for it. See dense and gummy sourdough.

Frequently asked questions

How long can sourdough cold proof in the fridge?

Many bakers cold proof for about 8 to 16 hours, and often up to 24 hours in a cold fridge. Longer is possible with a shorter bulk fermentation, but the risk of overproofing rises.

Can I bake sourdough straight from the fridge?

Yes. Most bakers do: cold dough is firmer, easier to score and holds its shape. Preheat the oven and pot well first.

How do I know when cold proofed sourdough is ready?

Use the poke test: if a floured fingertip dent springs back slowly and only partly, it is usually ready. If it springs back fast, give it more time; if it stays put, bake soon.

What temperature should my fridge be for cold proofing?

Around 38 to 40F (3 to 4C) keeps the dough nearly still. A warmer fridge keeps the dough rising, so shorten the cold proof.

Can I make sourdough ahead for Thanksgiving?

Yes. Mix and bulk ferment the day before, shape and refrigerate that evening, then bake the morning of. Feed your starter the night before you mix.
