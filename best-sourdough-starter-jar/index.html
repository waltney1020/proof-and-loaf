What makes a good sourdough starter jar

  • Straight sides. They let you judge the rise accurately (a rubber band at the starting level is the classic trick) and make scraping easy.
  • A wide mouth. Easy stirring, easy cleaning.
  • Room to grow. An active starter can double or more, so pick a jar at least three to four times the volume of starter you keep.
  • A loose lid. Fermentation makes gas. A lid that rests on top, or a screw lid left loose, is safer than an airtight seal.
  • Glass. See the bubbles, and glass doesn’t hold odors.

Starter jar habits that help

  • Move the starter to a clean jar every week or two to keep crust from building up.
  • Mark the level right after feeding so you can see the peak.
  • Keep a thermometer nearby: temperature explains most “my starter won’t rise” problems.

Frequently asked questions

What size jar do I need for a sourdough starter?

One that holds at least three to four times the starter you keep, so it can double or triple without overflowing. A 3/4 liter or quart jar suits most home bakers.

Should a sourdough starter jar be airtight?

No. Fermentation makes gas, so rest the lid on top or leave a screw lid loose. A sealed jar can build pressure.

Glass or plastic for a starter jar?

Glass is the favorite: you can see the bubbles, it doesn't hold odors, and it is easy to clean. Food-safe plastic is fine and won't break.

How often should I change my starter jar?

Move the starter to a clean jar every week or two, or whenever dried crust builds up on the sides.

Why is there condensation in my starter jar?

Warm, active starter releases moisture that collects on the lid and walls. It's normal and harmless.
