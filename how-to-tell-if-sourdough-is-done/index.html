How to Tell If Sourdough Bread Is Done (Temperature, Color and Sound)

Pulling a loaf too early is one of the most common beginner mistakes. Here are the checks that tell you it is really done, and what to do if it is not.

Quick answer

  • Internal temperature: most bakers treat about 205–210°F (96–99°C) in the center as done for a typical sourdough loaf.
  • Color: a deep golden to dark brown crust, darker on the ears, usually means a fully baked loaf. Pale often means underbaked.
  • Sound and feel: the bottom sounds hollow when tapped and the loaf feels light for its size.
  • Then wait: let it cool for at least 1–2 hours before slicing. The crumb is still setting, and cutting early can make a done loaf look gummy.

The most reliable check: internal temperature

Color and sound take practice to read. An instant-read thermometer gives you a number. Push the probe into the center of the loaf from the side or bottom (so you do not leave a hole on top), and read it once the number settles.

  • Under about 200°F (93°C): usually not done. Put it back in.
  • About 205–210°F (96–99°C): the range most sourdough recipes aim for.
  • Enriched or very wet doughs can need the upper end, or a few extra minutes after they reach it, so the crumb dries out enough.

Temperature tells you the inside is cooked, but it is not the whole story. A loaf can hit the number with a pale, soft crust, so use color as your second check.

Reading the crust color

Many beginners pull sourdough out too early because it already looks “golden”. A well-baked loaf is usually a deep, rich brown, and the darker color brings more flavor. If the top browns too fast while the inside is behind, lay a sheet of foil loosely over it for the rest of the bake.

The tap test

Hold the loaf with an oven mitt and tap the bottom. A hollow, drum-like sound suggests it is done; a dull thud suggests it needs more time. It is a useful habit, but it is less precise than a thermometer, especially for beginners.

Why waiting before you slice matters

Steam is still moving out of the crumb as the loaf cools, and the starches are still setting. Slicing a hot loaf lets that steam escape all at once and can leave the middle damp and gummy even when it was fully baked. Give it at least an hour or two on a wire rack. If your crumb still looks dense or wet after cooling, see why sourdough turns out dense and gummy.

Common reasons a loaf comes out underbaked

  • The oven runs cool. Many home ovens are off by a noticeable amount. An oven thermometer is a cheap way to find out.
  • The pot was not preheated long enough. Most Dutch oven recipes preheat for 30–45 minutes.
  • The lid stayed on too long, or came off too early. A common pattern is about 20 minutes covered, then 20–25 minutes uncovered; follow your recipe.
  • Underproofed or overproofed dough. Both can bake up tight or gummy. Our overproofing guide helps you tell the difference.

Can I put an underbaked loaf back in the oven?

Yes. If you catch it soon after baking, put it back in at your baking temperature, directly on the rack, for about 10 more minutes and check again. If it has fully cooled, a few minutes in the oven will still firm up the crust, though it will not fully fix a very wet center.

Frequently asked questions

What internal temperature should sourdough be when done?

Most bakers aim for about 205 to 210F (96 to 99C) in the center of the loaf. Below about 200F it is usually underbaked.

Can sourdough be gummy even when it is fully baked?

Yes. Slicing before the loaf has cooled for an hour or two lets steam escape and can make the crumb look gummy. Underproofing or overproofing can also leave a dense, damp crumb.

How dark should sourdough crust be?

Usually a deep golden to rich brown. A pale crust often means the loaf needed more time uncovered.

Does the hollow tap test work for sourdough?

It is a helpful sign, but less precise than a thermometer. A hollow sound from the bottom suggests the loaf is done; a dull thud suggests it needs more time.

Can I put underbaked sourdough back in the oven?

Yes. Put it back on the oven rack at baking temperature for about 10 minutes and check again. It works best soon after baking.
