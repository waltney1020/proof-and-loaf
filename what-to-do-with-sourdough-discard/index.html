Quick answer

  • Sourdough discard is the starter you pour off before a feed, so the jar doesn’t grow out of control and the fresh flour has less acid to fight.
  • It’s safe to bake with. Discard won’t reliably raise bread on its own, so most discard recipes also use baking soda, baking powder or a little yeast.
  • Keep it in the fridge in a jar with a loose lid. Many bakers use it within about a week or two and stir in any liquid on top.
  • Making less is the real fix: keep a smaller starter (10–50 g) and you’ll have far less to discard in the first place.

Why do you have to discard starter at all?

Each feed adds flour and water. If you never removed any, a 100 g starter fed 1:1:1 every day would weigh well over a kilo in a week, and you’d need more and more flour to keep it fed. Discarding keeps the starter a manageable size, and it keeps the ratio of fresh food to old, acidic starter high enough that the yeast stays lively.

You don’t have to throw it away, though. That’s why it’s called discard rather than waste.

How to store sourdough discard

  1. Use a dedicated jar in the fridge and add each day’s discard to it. Leave the lid loose, as it can still release a little gas.
  2. Write the start date on the jar. Older discard gets more sour, which some recipes welcome and others don’t.
  3. Stir in or pour off liquid that collects on top. That grey layer is normal, the same “hooch” a hungry starter makes.
  4. Throw it out if you see pink, orange or fuzzy mold, just as you would with a starter.

Many bakers find discard is best used within about one to two weeks. It can be frozen for longer; thaw it in the fridge and stir before using.

Easy ways to use sourdough discard

Discard works anywhere a recipe already has another way to rise, or doesn’t need to rise at all. These are the classic beginner uses; follow a recipe you trust for amounts and baking times.

  • Pancakes and waffles. Swap some of the flour and milk in a favorite recipe for discard; baking soda or powder still does the lifting.
  • Crackers. Discard, flour, oil or butter and salt, rolled thin and baked until crisp. Older, tangier discard suits these well.
  • Flatbreads and pizza. Add discard to a yeasted pizza or flatbread dough for extra flavor.
  • Quick breads and muffins. Banana bread, muffins and scones take discard easily as part of the liquid and flour.
  • Add it to a loaf for flavor. A spoonful in a yeasted sandwich loaf adds tang, though the yeast does the rising.
  • Dry it as a backup. Spread a thin layer on parchment, let it dry fully, and keep the flakes in a jar. Dried starter can be revived later, and it makes a nice homemade gift.

How to make less discard

  • Keep a smaller starter. You only need enough to feed. 10–50 g of starter is plenty for most home bakers.
  • Use a bigger ratio. Feeding 10 g of starter with 50 g each of flour and water (1:5:5) makes a full-strength starter with very little to pour off.
  • Move it to the fridge between bakes. A weekly feed means one small discard a week instead of one or two a day; see how to store starter in the fridge.
  • Build only what a recipe needs. The night before you bake, feed just enough for the recipe plus a spoonful to keep.

Frequently asked questions

What is sourdough discard?

It is the portion of starter you remove before a feed, so the starter stays a manageable size and the fresh flour isn't overwhelmed by old, acidic starter.

Why do you have to discard sourdough starter?

Without discarding, the starter keeps growing with every feed and needs more and more flour. Discarding keeps it small and lively.

How long does sourdough discard last in the fridge?

Many bakers use it within about one to two weeks, stirring in any liquid on top. Throw it out if you see pink, orange or fuzzy mold.

Can you use sourdough discard to make bread?

Discard usually won't raise a loaf reliably on its own. Add it to a yeasted dough for flavor, or use active starter for a true sourdough loaf.

How can I make less sourdough discard?

Keep a smaller starter (10 to 50 g), feed it at a bigger ratio like 1:5:5, and store it in the fridge between bakes.
