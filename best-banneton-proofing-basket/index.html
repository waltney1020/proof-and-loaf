What a banneton does

A banneton (also called a brotform or proofing basket) holds shaped dough during its final rise. It supports the sides so the loaf rises up rather than out, and the natural cane wicks a little moisture from the surface, which helps form a skin that’s easier to score. Food & Wine notes most baskets run 8–11 inches and hold roughly 500–1000 g of dough.

How to choose

  • Round or oval. Round for a boule in a round Dutch oven; oval for a batard. Match the shape to your baking vessel.
  • Size. A 9–10" basket fits a typical one-loaf recipe (about 500 g flour). Bigger loaves need bigger baskets.
  • Liner or no liner. Liners make release foolproof but you lose the spiral pattern. Many bakers use the liner while learning, then go bare with rice flour.

Care tips

  • Use rice flour. It doesn’t absorb water like wheat flour, so dough sticks far less.
  • Don’t wash it. Let it dry completely, then tap and brush out the flour. Moisture causes mold.
  • Never bake in it. Bannetons are for proofing only.
  • No banneton yet? A bowl lined with a well-floured, smooth kitchen towel works.

Frequently asked questions

What size banneton do I need?

For a standard loaf made with about 500 g of flour (roughly 900 g of dough), a 9 inch round or a 10 inch oval is the usual choice. Smaller loaves suit an 8 inch round.

Should I use the banneton liner?

Either works. A liner makes release easier and cleanup simpler, while a bare basket gives the classic flour rings. Dust well with rice flour either way.

How do I stop dough sticking to my banneton?

Dust with rice flour, which absorbs less water than wheat flour. Re-dust before every use, and don't over-proof very wet dough.

How do I clean a banneton and prevent mold?

Let it dry completely, then brush out the old flour. Never soak it or store it damp. A short time in a warm, switched-off oven helps it dry.

What can I use instead of a banneton?

A colander or bowl lined with a thin, well-floured linen or cotton tea towel works well.
